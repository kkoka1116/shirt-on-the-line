Gusting wind experiment

The additional four-second study uses the same changing wind for all three closures: a nominal 4 m/s front wind, smooth variations in strength, up to 10° of yaw, and 4° of pitch. Each cache was simulated at 40 substeps per frame with additional collision iterations. It retains the existing garment mesh and approximate aerodynamic model.

Comparing 20 and 40 substeps on the closed shirt changed mean vertex positions by 11.4 cm over this short run. This is a sensitivity check, not evidence of converged or measured fabric behavior. The original 18 cases remain available for comparison.

Appearance and physics

The natural ivory color is an approximate visual match to the Sabal undyed raw-silk shirt. The viewer uses fine procedural weave, restrained fiber sheen, and studio lighting. The material studies on the main page link to 4096 × 4096 Cycles path-traced renders, with finer irregular fibers, more matte highlights, and variation guided by the product photographs. These are appearance approximations, not scanned materials.

Blender cloth physics uses the reconstructed garment, source fabric density of 193.434 g/m², and 0.44 mm thickness. Wind is an approximate surface-pressure model; it does not solve wakes, airflow sheltering, permeability, or full fluid–cloth coupling.

This is a visual study. The detailed flutter is not numerically converged: doubling substeps in a five-second check changed the mean vertex positions by 9.4 cm. Local folds can intersect, and still air retains residual motion. Nominal wind speeds should not be treated as measured garment-response predictions.
