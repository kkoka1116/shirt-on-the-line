A shirt on the line.

18 independent cloth simulations compare three front-button configurations: all six closed, only body button #3 closed, and all six open. Numbering excludes the collar; cuffs stay closed and the collar stays open.

Choose still air, a nominal 2 m/s front wind, or a nominal 8 m/s wind from four directions. The shoulder supports are about 38.3 cm apart. Each case lasts 12 seconds, with 24 cached samples per second.

Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, or use the camera buttons. “Compare speeds” compares still air, 2 m/s, and 8 m/s for the selected button configuration.

Appearance and physics

The natural ivory color is an approximate visual match to the Sabal undyed raw-silk shirt. The viewer uses fine procedural weave, fiber sheen, and studio lighting.

Blender cloth physics uses the reconstructed garment, source fabric density of 193.434 g/m², and 0.44 mm thickness. Wind is an approximate surface-pressure model; it does not solve wakes, airflow sheltering, permeability, or full fluid–cloth coupling.

This is a visual study. The detailed flutter is not numerically converged: doubling substeps in a five-second check changed the mean vertex positions by 9.4 cm. Local folds can intersect, and still air retains residual motion. Nominal wind speeds should not be treated as measured garment-response predictions.
